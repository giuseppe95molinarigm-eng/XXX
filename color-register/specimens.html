Color Register · ten specimen entries

For approval before the other 185 entries are written. Each entry follows the same pattern:

  1. Background or overall layout first.
  2. Then stripes or blocks, in the order you read them: left to right, top to bottom.
  3. Then the emblem, from the biggest part to the smallest.
  4. Directions are always as you look at the page: left, right, top, bottom, middle.
Japan  A white flag with one red circle in the exact middle.
Why this one: The simplest case: one shape on a plain field. Sets the shortest entry length. (53 characters)
Egypt  Three equal stripes, top to bottom: red, white, black. In the middle of the white stripe stands a gold eagle looking to your left. On its chest is a small shield with upright stripes, left to right: red, white, black. Its claws hold a gold ribbon with the country's name in Arabic.
Why this one: Stripes plus an emblem inside a stripe; the emblem has its own small stripes, the opposite way round. (281 characters)
United States  Thirteen stripes, top to bottom, starting and ending with red: seven red and six white, taking turns. In the top left corner, a blue rectangle as tall as the top seven stripes, holding 50 small white stars in nine rows of six and five, taking turns.
Why this one: Counting: 13 stripes and 50 stars in alternating rows. Tests numbers in plain language. (249 characters)
Brazil  Green, with a large yellow diamond. Inside the diamond, a blue circle crossed by a curved white band, higher on the left than on the right, with the words ORDEM E PROGRESSO in green. Twenty-seven small white stars: one above the band, all the others below it.
Why this one: Shapes nested inside each other, a curved band with lettering, and stars of different sizes. (259 characters)
Belize  Royal blue, with a narrow red stripe along the top and the bottom. In the middle, a white circle ringed by 50 green leaves. Inside: a shield with tools in its two upper parts and, below, a sailing ship on a dark blue sea under a light blue sky. Two shirtless men in white trousers stand beside it, the left one holding an axe, the right one a paddle. A green mahogany tree rises above, and a scroll below reads SUB UMBRA FLOREO.
Why this one: The most detailed flag in the book: a full coat of arms with people. Tests the upper limit of entry length. (428 characters)

Nepal  Not a rectangle: two triangles stacked one on the other, the top one smaller, both pointing to your right. Crimson inside, with a blue border all the way around. In the top triangle, a white crescent moon lying on its back, with eight short rays rising from it; in the bottom triangle, a white sun with twelve points.
Why this one: The only flag that is not a rectangle. Tests describing shape before color. (317 characters)
Bhutan  Split by a straight line from the bottom left corner to the top right corner: the top half yellow, the bottom half orange. Across the line lies a white dragon facing right, holding a jewel in each claw.
Why this one: A diagonal division plus a large, intricate animal crossing the dividing line. (202 characters)
South Africa  A green band shaped like a Y lying on its side: its two arms start at the left corners, meet in the middle and run on as one band to the right edge. The Y has thin white edges on its outer sides and a thin gold edge on the inside. Inside the Y, on the left, a black triangle. Above the Y, red; below it, blue.
Why this one: Six colors, thin edging bands and a sideways Y. Tests describing narrow borders without flag terms. (309 characters)
Sri Lanka  A gold border runs around the flag and also splits it into two parts. On the left, two upright stripes: green, then orange. On the right, a large maroon panel with a gold lion facing left, a sword raised in its front paw, and a gold leaf in each corner.
Why this one: A frame that also divides the flag, two panels, and a figure with small corner details. (253 characters)
Saudi Arabia  Green. Across the upper middle runs a line of white Arabic writing, the declaration of faith; beneath it lies a white sword pointing to your left.
Why this one: An inscription. Tests how we describe writing the reader should not alter. (146 characters)
